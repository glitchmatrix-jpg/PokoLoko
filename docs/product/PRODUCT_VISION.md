# PokoLoko — Product Vision

## Core product promise

PokoLoko is a local-first desktop companion and ambient life-sim in which two distinct creatures—Poko and Loko—inhabit the edge of the user’s desktop with readable personalities, smooth physical continuity, contextual activities, and gentle autonomy. They walk, rest, play, read, use a laptop, listen to music, eat, drink, peek, sleep, and react to attention without demanding care, inspecting private content, or behaving like a random animation playlist. The product should feel less like software performing routines and more like a small, respectful life sharing the user’s space.

## Emotional target

**When PokoLoko closes, the desktop should feel a little quieter and a little emptier.**

## Experience pillars

### 1. Presence without pressure
The creatures may be noticed, ignored, paused, moved, or quietly observed. They never punish absence, display neglect meters, fake distress for engagement, or require daily maintenance.

### 2. Continuity before quantity
A smaller number of beautifully connected actions is better than many abrupt ones. Every activity is entered, performed, interrupted, and exited through posture-compatible choreography.

### 3. Recognizable individual character
Poko and Loko share a world but not a personality. Their action frequencies, pacing, attention styles, preferred activities, reactions, and recovery rhythms must differ enough that a user can identify them from behavior alone.

### 4. Context, never surveillance
Contextual behavior responds only to the presence and intensity of local activity signals—typing activity, pointer activity, system idle, audio playback, time, fullscreen state, lock, and resume. It does not inspect content.

### 5. Delight through legible surprise
Behavior should be probabilistic, history-aware, and personality-weighted. The user should recognize why an action was plausible without being able to predict every action.

### 6. The desktop as habitat
The pet respects work areas, taskbars, fullscreen applications, multiple displays, and user focus. Edge peeking, walking, settling, and play should feel tied to the physical desktop rather than rendered in an abstract box.

## Product identity

The authoritative visual identity uses the supplied PokoLoko logo system and final `pokoloko(1).ico` application icon. The interface should inherit its deep aubergine background, warm cream, charcoal, muted gold, restrained blush, rounded forms, and soft glow. It should feel intimate and polished, not toy-store loud or developer-panel sterile.

## Product architecture implied by the vision

The final experience requires separate but cooperating systems:

- **Pet state:** what the creature is physically doing.
- **Animation:** how the current action is drawn.
- **Locomotion:** where the creature moves on the desktop.
- **Mind:** hidden drives and mood tendencies.
- **Planner:** which legal intention becomes attractive next.
- **Context:** optional local activity signals.
- **Interaction:** clicks, repeated attention, dragging, tray actions, and quiet mode.
- **Session memory:** recent actions, interruptions, and rhythms.

No layer may bypass posture legality or directly swap incompatible visual states.

## Definition of “alive”

A PokoLoko creature feels alive when it:

- remains still long enough to feel comfortable rather than constantly animated;
- uses micro-idles irregularly instead of blinking on a metronome;
- begins activities for understandable reasons but not deterministic triggers;
- remembers what it has recently done and avoids obvious repetition;
- changes course safely when interrupted;
- has distinct pacing and preferences;
- appears grounded at all times;
- does not teleport between postures, directions, props, or locations;
- can be present for hours without becoming an obligation.

## Success statement

PokoLoko succeeds when users stop describing it as “an animated desktop pet” and begin describing what Poko or Loko “was doing” as though the action belonged to the character rather than to a timer.
