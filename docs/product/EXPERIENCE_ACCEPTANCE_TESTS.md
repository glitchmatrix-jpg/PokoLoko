# Experience Acceptance Tests

These tests are phrased as observable user experiences. Compilation and unit tests are necessary but insufficient.

## Presence

1. After launch, the selected creature appears cleanly above the taskbar with no white halo, clipping, blur, or visible scale jump.
2. During five minutes of observation, the creature can remain still naturally; it does not perform on a fixed metronome.
3. After thirty minutes, its behavior does not read as a repeating sequence of timer slots.
4. After two hours, the creature remains charming rather than demanding, frantic, or guilt-inducing.

## Character distinction

5. A tester watching muted recordings can correctly distinguish Poko from Loko by pacing and action choice without relying on color.
6. Poko more often expresses playful curiosity and short energetic bursts.
7. Loko more often settles into deliberate, sustained focus and quiet presence.
8. Loko occasionally plays and Poko occasionally focuses, so neither becomes a caricature.

## Context

9. Sustained typing makes laptop/reading behavior more plausible but does not trigger it every time or immediately.
10. With contextual awareness disabled, the pets remain fully functional and autonomous without reacting to typing or audio.
11. A privacy-conscious tester can explain what the app notices and confidently state that it does not read typed content.
12. Fullscreen use causes the pet to become unobtrusive according to the chosen setting.
13. After lock and resume, the pet returns gently without teleporting, racing, or replaying stale reactions.

## Activities

14. Laptop use begins from a compatible posture, includes setup, runs with variation, and ends without the laptop popping out of existence.
15. Reading follows the same continuity standard for the book.
16. Ball play introduces and removes the ball deliberately and can be interrupted safely by dragging.
17. Peeking occurs at a believable desktop edge or ledge, not in open space.
18. Eating and drinking preserve prop continuity and return to a valid stable posture.
19. Music feels character-specific and does not repeat so often that it becomes background noise.

## Movement and transitions

20. Walking visibly moves the pet across the desktop; the sprite never walks in place as the final behavior.
21. Foot cadence and travel speed feel coherent with no obvious sliding.
22. Turning does not produce an instantaneous one-frame mirror snap while moving.
23. Sleep is entered through a compatible transition and never jumps directly from standing to lying.
24. Waking never jumps directly from lying to walking.
25. Switching characters preserves ground position and does not flash a missing or stale frame.

## Interaction

26. Single click produces one timely, posture-compatible acknowledgment.
27. Repeated clicking does not queue a storm of reactions.
28. Poko and Loko respond differently to attention saturation.
29. Dragging immediately gives control to the pointer and stops autonomous movement.
30. Releasing over another display settles the pet on that display’s work-area ground without horizontal teleportation.
31. Dragging during sleep or an activity recovers to a valid state without a stuck prop or stale completion event.

## Controls and restraint

32. Pause stops autonomous behavior without making the app unresponsive to resume or quit.
33. Quiet mode produces visibly calmer behavior rather than merely muting sound.
34. Reduced motion lowers movement and transition intensity while preserving usability.
35. Size changes use crisp integer scaling and do not alter relative frame size.
36. Tray and settings state always match the actual running pet.
37. The app can be closed without emotional pressure or manipulative messaging.

## Emotional target

38. After a long successful session, closing PokoLoko makes the desktop feel subtly emptier—not because the app demanded attention, but because a coherent presence is gone.
