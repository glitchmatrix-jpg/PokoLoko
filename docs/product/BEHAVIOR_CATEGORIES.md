# Behavior Categories

## FOUNDATIONAL

Actions required for continuous physical existence and legal posture flow.

Includes:

- stable idle;
- walking and direction;
- stopping and settling;
- sitting/resting where supported;
- sleep entry, sleep loop, and wake recovery;
- dragging and landing recovery;
- character switching reset.

Foundational behavior has priority over novelty. No activity may destabilize it.

## CONTEXTUAL

Actions whose likelihood is influenced by local, optional context.

Examples:

- laptop during sustained typing;
- reading during quiet study;
- music when audio playback is present;
- rest during system idle;
- peeking near an actual screen edge;
- reduced activity during fullscreen use.

Contextual actions remain probabilistic and cooldown-protected.

## SPONTANEOUS

Self-initiated actions that express personality independent of immediate user input.

Examples:

- Poko music or ball play;
- Loko’s rare ball play;
- exploratory walking;
- eating or drinking;
- short curiosity routines.

Spontaneous activity must respect recent history and user activity level.

## SOCIAL

Actions triggered by direct user attention or the social history of the current session.

Examples:

- click acknowledgment;
- affection;
- mild annoyance after saturation;
- drag pickup and release response;
- rare contextual sadness, never neglect punishment.

Social responses are coalesced and cooldown-limited.

## AMBIENT

Small actions that make stillness feel alive without demanding attention.

Examples:

- irregular blink;
- glance;
- ear or posture shift from validated isolated poses;
- quiet breathing sleep variation;
- brief orientation after resume.

Ambient behavior is sparse. Still frames and long holds are part of the design.

## Activity experience contract

Every nontrivial activity must define:

1. eligibility conditions;
2. starting posture;
3. travel or placement requirement;
4. entry choreography;
5. prop setup;
6. main loop or phrase list;
7. small variations;
8. minimum and maximum duration;
9. soft and immediate interruption behavior;
10. prop cleanup;
11. exit choreography;
12. resulting posture;
13. cooldown;
14. drive and mood effects.

An animation ID alone is not an activity implementation.
